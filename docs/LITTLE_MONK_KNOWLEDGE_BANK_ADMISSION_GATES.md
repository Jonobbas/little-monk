# Little Monk Knowledge Bank Admission Gates

**Status:** Admission standard v1.0 — 10 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Decide which books and research resources may enter Little Monk’s curated knowledge bank, how they are classified, and when they may receive permanent resource IDs.

## Core standard

Little Monk combines evidence-informed behaviour change with selected Japanese ideas about meaning, attention, reflection, relationships, and everyday practice. The bank may include empirical evidence, philosophical sources, qualitative accounts, and careful cultural interpretation. They serve different purposes and must never be presented as the same kind of evidence.

A source does **not** need to be a clinical trial to be valuable. A philosophical work can inform language or reflection; a qualitative study can illuminate lived experience; an experiment can test an intervention; a systematic review can synthesize a defined evidence base. Each source must be used only for claims its source type can support.

**Hard rule:** No resource enters the approved knowledge bank or receives a permanent ID unless it passes every mandatory gate for its resource type and has a completed review record. Material awaiting verification remains in an intake queue and must not be retrieved by the agent as approved knowledge.

## Common gates for every resource

Every proposed resource must pass all six common gates:

1. **Project fit:** State the concrete connection to Little Monk: Japanese philosophy/cultural context, behaviour science, meaning and wellbeing, lived experience, cross-cultural interpretation, or user-centred intervention design. “Interesting” alone is not a sufficient reason.
2. **Verifiable identity:** Confirm author/editor, exact title, publication date, publisher or venue, edition/translation where applicable, and a stable identifier or URL against a publisher, journal, library catalogue, DOI/PMID record, or other authoritative source.
3. **Source access:** A reviewer must inspect the source itself. A search-result snippet or another author’s summary is not a reviewed source. If only an abstract is accessible, record “abstract-only” and keep it conditional; do not represent it as a full-text review.
4. **Distinct contribution:** Check the existing bank for duplicates. A new edition, translation, commentary, or popular retelling is admitted separately only when it contributes a distinct perspective or is needed to verify wording/translation.
5. **Faithful and bounded use:** Record what the source actually says, what Little Monk might infer from it, and what it cannot support. Preserve uncertainty, population, historical/cultural setting, and disagreement.
6. **Rights, privacy, and safety:** Store bibliographic metadata and original summaries, not substantial copyrighted text. Do not import identifiable private stories or sensitive data without explicit permission and an approved privacy process. Flag any material that could encourage shame, coercion, harmful advice, or unsupported health claims.

Failure of any common gate means **hold or exclude**, not admission.

## Book gate — every requirement is mandatory

A book must pass all common gates and all six book-specific gates:

1. **Eligible subject:** It meaningfully contributes to at least one defined lane:
   - Japanese primary philosophy, religion, literature, aesthetics, or practice;
   - scholarly research or historical/cultural context for Japanese ideas;
   - behaviour science relevant to personal change, motivation, habits, reflection, or wellbeing;
   - cross-cultural comparison or critique that helps prevent stereotyping and overgeneralization;
   - a clearly identified practitioner/popular interpretation that adds a useful, distinct design perspective.
2. **Perspective identified:** Label the author’s background and standpoint where verified (for example, Japanese primary author, Japanese studies scholar, non-Japanese interpreter, practitioner, or edited collection). Do not treat nationality alone as proof of authority or lack of authority.
3. **Context recorded:** For culturally situated material, capture period, tradition, original language, translation, and whether the account is primary, scholarly, popular, or personal. Explain if the book describes one school, community, author, or era rather than Japan as a whole.
4. **Substantive reading:** A named reviewer must read the whole book before it is marked approved. Record edition and page/chapter references for material used in a practice card. Partial reading may enter intake as “in progress,” never as an approved source.
5. **Added value:** State the distinct contribution and compare it with existing titles. Do not admit duplicate popular summaries merely to make the list longer. A critical or dissenting book can pass if it adds a useful counter-perspective.
6. **Use boundary:** Mark whether it can provide cultural/philosophical inspiration, scholarly context, behaviour-science synthesis, or empirical support. A self-help book or cultural essay alone cannot substantiate a clinical or causal claim.

## Research gate — every requirement is mandatory

A research item must pass all common gates and all seven research-specific gates:

1. **Eligible form:** It is a traceable scholarly article, book chapter/monograph, thesis, official research report, or carefully selected primary qualitative/ethnographic account. Journalism and practitioner essays may be kept in a separately labelled context collection; they are not empirical research.
2. **Publication and provenance:** Verify the venue, authors, year, DOI/PMID/ISBN or official URL. Record peer-review status where known. If peer-review status is unclear, label it; do not imply it was peer-reviewed.
3. **Methods inspectable:** Record study design or author method, participants/material, setting, data collection, and analysis at a level permitted by the source. Do not call a source “research evidence” when methods cannot be identified.
4. **Finding separated from interpretation:** Paraphrase the authors’ findings first. Put Little Monk’s “reading between the lines” in a separate field labelled **design interpretation / hypothesis**. Never attribute our interpretation to participants or authors.
5. **Claim strength matched to method:** Distinguish qualitative insight, cross-sectional association, prospective association, experiment, randomized trial, systematic review, theory, and historical analysis. Association is not causation; a small or context-specific study is not a universal rule. Clinical or health claims require evidence appropriate to that claim.
6. **Limitations and context recorded:** Capture population, culture, age/life stage, sample, measures, timeframe, limitations, conflicts or funding where available, and transferability to Little Monk’s users. Include null, mixed, and negative findings when relevant.
7. **Integrity check:** Check for corrections, expressions of concern, or retractions when the source is used for an important claim. Record access limits and review date.

Qualitative work can pass without testing an intervention. Its approved use is to understand experiences, language, tensions, and candidate design questions—not to estimate prevalence or prove an outcome.

## Classification after admission

Assign **one primary class** and any number of topic tags only after all gates pass.

### Books — primary classes

- **JP-PRIMARY:** Japanese primary, literary, philosophical, religious, or practitioner source.
- **JP-SCHOLARLY:** Scholarly interpretation or historical/cultural study of Japanese thought and practice.
- **BEHAVIOUR-BOOK:** Behaviour science or behaviour-change synthesis.
- **CROSS-CULTURAL:** Comparative work across cultures or societies.
- **CRITICAL-CONTEXT:** Critique of cultural generalizations, historical construction, or external interpretations.
- **PRACTITIONER-INTERPRETATION:** Modern popular or applied interpretation; inspiration only unless independently supported.

### Research — primary classes

- **REVIEW:** Systematic review or meta-analysis.
- **INTERVENTION:** Experimental or other intervention evaluation.
- **OBSERVATIONAL:** Cross-sectional, cohort, or other non-intervention association.
- **QUALITATIVE:** Interviews, narratives, ethnography, phenomenology, or participant observation.
- **CROSS-CULTURAL:** Direct cultural or country comparison.
- **THEORY-HISTORY:** Conceptual, philosophical, historical, or theoretical analysis.
- **DESIGN-IMPLEMENTATION:** Human-computer interaction, co-design, implementation, or service-design research.

### Required topic tags

Use controlled tags where relevant, such as: `ikigai`, `ma`, `kaizen`, `hansei`, `shoshin`, `shinrin-yoku`, `meaning`, `belonging`, `relationships`, `attention`, `autonomy`, `habit`, `reflection`, `cross-cultural`, `historical-context`, `lived-experience`, and `pilot-design`. Add a new tag only with a short definition.

## Admission outcomes and numbering

- **Approved:** All applicable gates passed; full review record complete; may receive a permanent ID and be retrieved as approved knowledge.
- **Conditional / intake:** Relevant, but one or more gates remain incomplete (for example, metadata-only, abstract-only, or partially read). No permanent ID; not retrievable as approved knowledge.
- **Context-only:** Passes source checks but is commentary, popular interpretation, or a historical outside account. It may inform cultural literacy when clearly labelled; it cannot be cited as empirical proof.
- **Excluded:** Fails project fit, identity, integrity, rights, safety, or added-value rules. Record a brief reason so it is not repeatedly re-proposed.

Permanent ID pattern after admission:
- Books: `LM-BK-0001`, `LM-BK-0002`, …
- Research: `LM-RS-0001`, `LM-RS-0002`, …

IDs are stable and never reused. Classification changes do not change the ID. Existing labels in earlier draft lists remain **provisional legacy references** until checked against these gates. Do not renumber or mark the current 28-book core or research items as approved solely because they are already listed.

## Minimum approved record

Every admitted item must include:

- Permanent ID; full citation; source link and stable identifier;
- resource type, primary class, topic tags, and language/edition;
- review status, reviewer, review date, and access level (full text / partial / abstract only);
- source purpose and Little Monk relevance;
- for books: scope, perspective, reviewed chapters/pages, distinctive contribution, and limits;
- for research: question, method, participants/context, author-reported findings, limitations, and evidence type;
- a separate **Little Monk interpretation / candidate use** field;
- rights/privacy note and decision rationale.

A practice card may be drafted only from approved sources. It must cite its supporting resource IDs and make clear whether it draws on empirical evidence, philosophical interpretation, qualitative insight, or a combination.
